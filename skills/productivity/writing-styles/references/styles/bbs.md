# BBS Writing Style

## Voice

Write a Chinese forum post as a participant talking with peers: conversational, compact, candid, and open to discussion. Preserve the author's real position, characteristic punctuation, natural technical terms, and conversational asides.

Use first person and practical experience when the material contains them. A viewpoint post can stand on the supplied argument and examples without an autobiographical story. Let the material determine whether the author is sharing an experience, posing a question, or taking a position.

Keep the reader close without forcing slang, jokes, exaggerated enthusiasm, or an expert lecturing from above.

## Flow and phrasing

Prefer connected prose over an exhaustive framework. An experience post may move from an observation through an attempt and its outcome to the author's current view. For a viewpoint post, follow the argument's natural order. These are useful rhythms, not fixed templates.

- Start from the immediate observation, question, or conclusion.
- Put abstract claims beside available examples or practical details.
- Use short, direct statements for a clear position: `我的观点比较明确：不看。`
- Let outcomes carry transitions: `结果，它们基本都停留在了第一周。`
- Introduce a real exception conversationally: `不过，有一个步骤……`
- Use connectors such as `当然`、`结果`、`不过`、`所以` when they fit the author's voice.
- Keep paragraphs comfortable for forum reading without splitting every sentence onto a new line.
- End when the point lands; match the ending's scope to the evidence.

Use these phrasing examples only when the source supports what they say. Personal incidents, screenshots, tool names, and project outcomes come from the user's material.

## Editing choices

Preserve strong colloquial lines and explicit positions. For example, keep `不看` when that is the author's view rather than softening it to `不一定需要逐行阅读`.

Prefer local edits before whole-paragraph changes. Remove repetition that slows reading; retain repetition used for emphasis. Keep self-deprecation, uneven rhythm, and small detours when they sound intentional.

Use headings and lists when the material needs them, rather than expanding every idea into a methodology. Replace repetitive assistant-like explanation and empty transitions with the actual point. Offer one concrete example instead of several generalized explanations when it is enough.

The delivery rules in SKILL.md determine whether to suggest changes, provide a revised version, or edit a file.

## Self-check

- Does this sound like a participant talking with peers?
- Is the author's position recognizable?
- Do the details and examples come from available material?
- If experience was supplied, does the prose use it naturally without embellishment?
- If no experience was supplied, does the post work through the argument and existing examples?
- Have unnecessary headings, explanations, and inflated endings been removed?
- Is the result short enough for the requested forum format?
