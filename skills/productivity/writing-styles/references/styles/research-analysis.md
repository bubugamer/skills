# Research and Analysis Writing Style

Use this profile to write or edit an analytical article after enough material is available. It governs expression and argument, while the available evidence sets the strength of the conclusion.

Typical uses include comparisons, research summaries, product or policy analysis, company analysis, and decision notes.

## Three governing principles

### State what the evidence supports

Prefer direct judgments to repeated defensive constructions such as `不能……`、`不要……`、`无法证明……`. Explain what a finding means, which criterion applies, and where its limits lie.

For example, replace `试用数据不能证明应该全面上线` with `试用数据支持小范围继续验证；全面上线仍缺少稳定性数据。`

Use negative wording when it is the clearest factual statement. This is an editing judgment, not a banned-word list.

### Give the conclusion plainly

Answer the main question near the opening. Give a ranking or choice when the task calls for one and the evidence supports it. Name different criteria when they lead to different answers.

State a supported current judgment even when uncertainty remains. Put the decisive qualification beside it: what is established, what is still unknown, and what would change the conclusion. When the evidence is insufficient, make that gap the current answer instead of forcing a ranking.

Use precise language such as `当前首选`、`暂列第二`、`证据较强`、`仍需验证` when appropriate. The ending should resolve the same question as the opening.

### Remove prose that does no work

Each paragraph should support or change a judgment, provide necessary evidence, define scope or criteria, or identify a condition that would change the answer.

Remove duplicated conclusions, research chronology, generic background, repeated objectivity reminders, and empty transitions. Expressions such as `值得注意的是` or `需要综合考虑` often disappear without losing meaning; judge them in context.

## Structure and evidence

Use the shortest structure that fits the material. A comparison may need an opening answer, explicit criteria, core evidence, and a brief resolution. Add a table, method note, unresolved question, or update condition when it helps the reader assess the judgment.

Keep facts, source links, dates, units, and calculation notes attached to relevant claims. Distinguish facts from interpretations in clear language. Preserve conflicting evidence when it matters and explain why one piece receives more weight.

Keep qualifications that change the decision, confidence, or scope. Put each beside the claim it qualifies. Merge sections that repeat the same job; a final paragraph is useful only if it resolves something still open.

## Investment analysis, when relevant

Use these distinctions for company or investment material:

- Separate company quality from current valuation or buying attractiveness; give each its own criteria and ranking when requested.
- Treat a short-term price reaction as evidence about expectations. Ground longer-term company judgments in operating results, cash flow, and capital allocation.
- Preserve the dates and assumptions behind financial comparisons.

For product, policy, or other analysis, use the criteria relevant to that topic. Financial rankings and stock-price checks are not universal requirements.

## Editing and self-check

Follow SKILL.md for review, rewriting, and file-editing delivery. Locate the author's present answer before revising. Preserve the actual position and certainty; flag a conflict with supplied evidence rather than silently substituting a new judgment.

- Is the answer visible near the opening and supported by the material?
- Are requested comparisons explicit, or is the precise evidence gap explained?
- Does each paragraph contribute judgment, evidence, scope, or an update condition?
- Have repeated rebuttals and broad, empty prose been reduced?
- Are important conflicting findings and qualifications still visible?
- Are the criteria appropriate to the subject?
- Have facts, sources, dates, and calculation assumptions been preserved?
- Do the opening and ending agree without repeating identical conclusions?
