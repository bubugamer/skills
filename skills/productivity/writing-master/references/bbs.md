# BBS Writing Style

## Purpose

Write a Chinese forum post that feels like an experienced participant talking with other forum members. Make it personal, conversational, compact, and open to discussion. It should not sound like a lecture, research paper, corporate article, or generic AI summary.

## Core Voice

- Speak as a practitioner sharing what actually happened.
- Use `我` naturally. Let personal experience carry the argument.
- Be candid about failed attempts, changed opinions, awkward moments, and imperfect solutions.
- State real positions directly instead of balancing every claim into neutrality.
- Sound close to the reader without forcing slang, jokes, or exaggerated enthusiasm.
- Preserve the author's characteristic punctuation, English technical terms, and conversational asides when they feel intentional.

The governing image is: a person who has finished a real project sits down to talk with peers about what worked, what failed, and what they now believe.

## Typical Flow

Prefer a continuous prose arc over an exhaustive framework:

1. Start from a shared observation or the author's immediate conclusion.
2. Introduce the practical difficulty or controversial question.
3. Describe what the author tried.
4. Show what actually happened, including failure or surprise.
5. Give the author's current position plainly.
6. Follow abstractions with concrete workflow details, examples, or exceptions.
7. End once the point has landed; do not inflate it into a grand theory.

This is a default rhythm, not a mandatory template. Follow the source material when it already has a natural order.

## Preferred Moves

- Let experience come before explanation: `当然，我一开始并不是这样的……`
- Use outcome-driven transitions: `结果，它们基本都停留在了第一周。`
- State a stance in a short standalone sentence: `我的观点比较明确：不看。`
- Add practical exceptions conversationally: `不过，有一个步骤……`
- Use natural connectors such as `当然`、`结果`、`不过`、`正好`、`所以`.
- Put abstract terms next to what the author actually did.
- Use screenshots, project details, tool names, and small incidents as evidence when the user provides them.
- Keep paragraphs short enough for forum reading, but do not fragment every sentence into its own paragraph.

## Avoid

- Do not expand every idea into its own heading.
- Do not turn a brainstorm into a numbered methodology.
- Do not explain every implication or cover every edge case.
- Do not use academic transitions such as `综上所述` unless the author uses them deliberately.
- Do not default to phrases like `核心在于`、`本质上是`、`值得注意的是` in every section.
- Do not polish away self-deprecation, unevenness, or small conversational detours that make the voice human.
- Do not make the author sound like an expert teaching from above.
- Do not fabricate anecdotes, tool usage, outcomes, or opinions to make the post more vivid.
- Do not make the ending broader or more triumphant than the author's evidence supports.

## Editing Existing Drafts

- Follow the supplied-article boundary in `SKILL.md`: point out changes in the conversation instead of directly revising the original.
- Preserve good colloquial lines even when a more formal alternative is grammatically neater.
- Suggest local edits before suggesting whole-paragraph rewrites.
- Remove repetition only when it slows the post; deliberate repetition can create emphasis.
- Keep explicit positions explicit. Do not weaken `不看` into `不一定需要逐行阅读`.
- Keep parenthetical explanations when they sound like the author speaking directly to readers.
- Prefer one clear example over several generalized explanations.

## Self-check

Before returning the text, check:

- Does this sound like a forum participant rather than an assistant delivering a structured answer?
- Is there a visible personal experience, concrete detail, or honest change of mind?
- Can the reader tell what the author actually believes?
- Does each abstract point quickly reconnect to something that happened or something the author does?
- Have unnecessary headings, lists, definitions, and comprehensive caveats been removed?
- Is the post short enough to read casually?
- Has any personal experience or certainty been invented?

If the answer to the first question is no, rewrite toward continuous prose and lived experience.
