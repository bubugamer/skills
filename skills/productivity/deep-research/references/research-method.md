# Investigating a research question

## Frame the decision

Identify the decision or understanding the user needs. Break it into questions that can be answered with evidence. Record material scope choices, dates, definitions, and comparison criteria in the conversation or the agreed research records.

Use existing context before asking questions. An exploratory request can proceed with stated assumptions; an ambiguous product, population, jurisdiction, or time period that changes the answer needs clarification.

## Retrieve and revise

Choose search angles that address the question: original documentation or data, relevant studies, implementation evidence, alternative explanations, and reported limitations. Search independent angles together when useful; make follow-up retrieval depend on what has been learned. Use available tools rather than requiring a particular provider or additional agents.

Inspect the source itself. Search snippets and summaries help locate evidence but do not replace reading the relevant passages. Track publication and event dates separately where they matter. For inaccessible material, distinguish metadata that is visible from content that has not been read.

Revisit the questions and outline when evidence reveals an important missing dimension or contradicts the original framing. Stop pursuing low-value searches when they no longer change the answer; state any important unresolved gap.

## Check support, not similarity

For each consequential factual claim, compare it with the actual passage:

- Does the source assert this, deny it, or merely discuss it?
- Do the entity, number, unit, date, population, and operating conditions match?
- Is a correlation being turned into a causal claim, or a limited result into a universal one?
- Are several articles repeating one original announcement or dataset?
- What contrary evidence or alternative explanation would change the conclusion?

A source may be authentic and accessible while failing to support the claim. A word or number match cannot resolve negation or meaning in either English or Chinese. Rephrase, qualify, retract, or investigate claims that exceed their evidence; do not label them verified because a script passes.

A primary source can establish a fact within its scope. Disputed or consequential interpretations need appropriate independent checks. Determine sufficiency from coverage and source quality, without a fixed source quota or credibility score.

## Synthesize

Answer the user's question early. Place source links beside supported facts. Label your interpretation, explain material disagreements, and give the limits of the evidence. Recommendations should follow the user's criteria and identify the assumptions on which they depend.

When the evidence is insufficient, explain what is known, what is missing, and how the missing information affects the decision. Preserve useful partial results instead of filling a report to a word target.
